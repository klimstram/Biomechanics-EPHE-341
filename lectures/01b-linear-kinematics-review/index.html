Kinematics

Kinematics:

The study of motion exclusive of the influences of mass and force. It includes displacement, velocity, and acceleration without regard for the forces acting on a body.

the branch of biomechanics that deals with the description of the spatial and temporal components of motion

Three important Questions:

  1. How far did an object/person travel?
  2. How fast did the object/person travel?
  3. Was the object/person moving faster at some points of the movement and slower at others?

In Biomechanics it is also important to consider the movement of the individuals limb segments

Source slide 3. The three questions are the lecture in order: distance and displacement, speed and velocity, acceleration.

Kinematics

Types of Motion

1.  Linear Motion

  • uniform motion of all parts of an object in the same direction and at the same speed
  • more commonly known as translation
  • does not necessarily mean motion in a straight line:
    straight line → rectilinear
    curved line → curvilinear
Source slide 5.

Kinematics

Types of Motion

2.  Angular Motion

  • rotation around an axis (axis can be real or imaginary)
  • different parts of the object undergo different linear displacements
  • all parts of the object undergo the same angular displacement
  • for example, the ankle travels at a greater curvilinear speed than the knee
  • both the ankle and the knee travel at the same angular speed
Source slide 6. The figure is the leg turning about the hip: the same angle at the knee and the ankle, a longer arc at the ankle in the same time.

3.  General Motion

  • a combination of linear and angular motion

Position - space

  • What about GPS?
    • Where we are on earth?

The Global Positioning System (GPS) is a network of about 30 satellites orbiting the Earth at an altitude of 20,000 km.

If you know how far away you are from satellite A, then you know you must be located somewhere on the red circle. If you do the same for satellites B and C, you can work out your location by seeing where the three circles intersect. This is just what your GPS receiver does, although it uses overlapping spheres rather than circles.

The more satellites there are above the horizon the more accurately your GPS unit can determine where you are.

Source slide 20, and its speaker note: GPS satellites circle the earth twice a day in a very precise orbit and transmit signal information to earth. GPS receivers take this information and use trilateration to calculate the user’s exact location. Essentially, the GPS receiver compares the time a signal was transmitted by a satellite with the time it was received. The time difference tells the GPS receiver how far away the satellite is. Now, with distance measurements from a few more satellites, the receiver can determine the user’s position and display it on the unit’s electronic map. A GPS receiver must be locked on to the signal of at least three satellites to calculate a 2D position (latitude and longitude) and track movement. With four or more satellites in view, the receiver can determine the user’s 3D position (latitude, longitude and altitude). Once the user’s position has been determined, the GPS unit can calculate other information, such as speed, bearing, track, trip distance, distance to destination, sunrise and sunset time and more.

Solution

Displacement is the change in position, measured from the initial position to the final position. Therefore, her displacement is:

Vectors

  • First we will deal with the simplest situation of collinear vectors
  • two vectors are collinear if they are parallel
  • The two vectors, A and B are collinear and may be added to form a resultant
  • collinear vectors can be added using simple arithmetic, or scalar addition
  • the direction of the resultant vector is the same as that of vectors A and B

∣A⃗∣+∣B⃗∣=∣R⃗∣|\vec{A}| + |\vec{B}| = |\vec{R}|∣A∣+∣B∣=∣R∣

Infrared timing gates

Photogates use the same technology that your TV remote uses. On one side of the small slot is an LED (like a small light bulb) that emits a beam of infrared light. Instantaneous vs. Average Values

  • What we have been calculating are averages over a certain period of time
  • instantaneous values can be thought of as average values that are calculated over infinitely small periods of time (i.e. as close as the time that you use to calculate the average t2−t1t_2 - t_1t2​−t1​ can get to being 0)
  • an average value does not provide accurate information about rate of change
  • for example, Bolt’s velocity and acceleration were likely to have been changing within each 50 m section of the 100 m race, however, we only have one average value the whole race.
